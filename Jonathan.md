## 🎯 How to Approach Jonathan — Read This First

**He's intellectually engaged, physically capable, mentally guarded.** Everything that limits him right now lives in his head, not his body. He has strong arms, abs, leg flexibility. He sees what's happening, can describe it precisely, even uses words like "cohesive perception of position." Trust his self-awareness — talk to it.

- **Frontside vs backside is the key divide.** Frontside he flows beautifully. Backside he's robotic, gets stuck, hand drops, can't pop-up cleanly. **Treat them as two separate teaching tracks.** Use frontside wins to build backside courage.
- **Pop-up = mental release, not strength.** Body has everything. The work is: release the hands, jump, trust the landing. Drill in low-stakes settings until the release becomes automatic.
- **He's tight.** He even named it: "I'm 'too tight' — I'm just an hour into something tai-chi people have done for 200 years." Honor that with patience AND with concrete mobility/looseness work (dry-land flow, carver play, stretching).
- **Carver translates for him.** The carver work has already helped his frontside flow significantly. Use the carver for between-session reinforcement, especially for backside off-the-top reps.
- **Wave selection is a recurring weakness.** Position has always been hard for him. The first 2–3 days, focus less on maneuvers and more on WHERE he should be sitting — relative to the peak, the shoulder, the foam.
- **Shortboard volume:** he's on a board near the bottom limit of what his weight allows. You support that, but it means he has zero margin — calm paddling and exact positioning are non-negotiable. Manage this with him.
- **Reef break is his happy place.** Plan around that when conditions allow.

## Roadmap — What to Focus On

**Priority 1 — Wave selection / positioning first.** (his historical weakness + Day 1 problem)
- 4m of wall breaking in front = walk away. He doesn't have a longboard's speed reserve to traverse it.
- He wants the section where ~1m is about to break next to him.
- Days 1–3 focus: less maneuvers, more "where to sit, when to take it."

**Priority 2 — Come back via rotation, not crouch.**
- When the wall fades, don't crouch looking for power. Rotate back toward the whitewater — toe-side rotation, soft.
- Pressure + rotation on the back foot → back to the foam (BEHIND him on a point).
- Cue: "Power isn't down. Power is the spin back."

**Priority 3 — Pop-up: mental release work (backside priority).**
- Physical capacity is there. Work is releasing hands, committing, trusting the landing.
- Drill: 10 backside pop-ups on land before each session, last 3 with eyes closed to practice trust.
- In-water: keep him on easier waves until the release is automatic, then progress.

**Priority 4 — Stay in the middle band, not top, not bottom.**
- Soft turns, lots of them, between upper and lower sections.
- Cue: "Below the top, above the bottom — small smooth arcs."

**Priority 5 — Carver between sessions.**
- Reinforces flow, especially backside. Use it during rest periods.

**Priority 6 — Tightness work.**
- Daily mobility/stretching — non-negotiable, especially given his self-acknowledged tightness.

**Priority 7 — More wave time = more mastery.**
- The "boring, unimpressive" feel of small smooth arcs IS the path. Longer wave time → comfort → mastery.
- Cue: "Boring is the goal right now."

### Working hypothesis on level
Solid intermediate with a beautiful natural style. Ceiling is mental and tactical (selection, release, softness), not physical. Backside is the obvious growth axis. If pop-up releases AND he picks softer entries, he jumps a level on this trip.
