## 🎯 How to Approach Roee — Read This First

**⚠️ INJURY-FIRST. Then teach.** Roee has a serious shoulder injury (tendinosis — small tears in the shoulder tendons from past over-use). He's in ongoing physiotherapy. **Manage his session intensity before anything else.** Day 1 session 1 felt 7/10 effort for him. 7/10 × 2 sessions × 10 days = injury. Keep him at ~5/10.

- **Volume strategy:** shorter water time (1.5h instead of 2.5h), longer rest between sessions, fewer waves chased, patient wave selection (1 wave / 15–20 min is fine).
- **Stretching is mandatory** after every session. Do the first one WITH him so it sticks. If he skips, call it out.
- **Shortboard temptation:** he wants to step up to a shortboard during the trip. Hold him back. "If it feels right, do it — but don't push it."
- **He'll push himself past pain.** Be the brake.

**On the teaching side, Roee is theory-rich, execution-poor.** He watches a lot of surfing video, knows the moves intellectually, but doesn't apply them in the water. He explicitly asked: **"I just need someone to tell me 'you're not doing X, focus on this on the next wave.'"**

- **One cue per wave** — that's his learning channel.
- **Always explain the WHY.** He doesn't want to do moves "just because" — he wants to understand the reasoning so he can apply it himself. Frame every drill: "we're doing this because…"
- **His core skill goal: speed preservation.** He loses speed on his bottom turn and the wave dies. He explicitly identified this as his #1 wish. Hammer it.
- **His core knowledge goal: wave reading.** When to bottom turn, when to top turn, when to wait.
- **He gets in his own head** — over-thinks mid-wave. Counter with simple, direct cues.

## Day 1 — Wave Analysis Summary

### What I saw
- Generates good speed, picks good waves, the wall stood for him the whole way.
- BUT — he's copy-pasting the same line. Same movement, same rhythm, every wave.
- When the wall goes flat he keeps running instead of pausing and reconnecting — a small cutback (or even just turning back) would unlock the second half.
- On double-up waves (the best walls available) he's working hard but not connecting — needs to lean in hard and stick to them.
- Energy issue: bending into the rotation correctly, but then scraping the hand and pushing through the toes = energy stays trapped DOWN. Needs to release energy UP through extension after the compression.
- Stance is too far forward — his back is too forward, turning options minimal.

### What's working
- Strong speed generation.
- Good wave selection.
- Has the bend / compression piece.

### What's blocking progression
- One-trick rhythm — same line every wave.
- Energy stops at the bottom (no extension up).
- Feet too far forward — turning options choked.
- Doesn't yet recognize when to wait, when to go back.

## Roadmap — What to Focus On

**Priority 0 (always-on) — Injury management.**
- Cap intensity at ~5/10. Long rests. Short sessions. Stretch after every session.
- Hold him back from shortboard until end of trip — IF at all.

**Priority 1 — Decision-making on a flat wall.** (serves his wave-reading goal)
- When the wall goes flat: pause. Turn back. Reconnect to the foam. Don't keep running.
- Cue: "When it dies, go get it back."
- Drill: pick one wave per session where he commits to a turn-back instead of running.

**Priority 2 — Walk the feet BACK before the turn.**
- His stance is too forward — back foot slides back, front foot adjusts, BEFORE the turn.
- Cue: "Tap-tap fast, then turn."

**Priority 3 — Release energy UP through extension.** (serves his speed-preservation goal)
- After the bend → chest rises, knees extend, hands push up.
- Cue: "Compress, then send it up."

**Priority 4 — Try a longboard / glider for cutback practice.**
- Gives him the time and forgiveness to feel the cutback rhythm.

**Priority 5 — Lean in HARD on the double-ups.**
- Best walls on offer. He's working hard but not committing the angle.

### Sequencing note (his own concern)
He asked "when do I move my feet?" mid-debrief. Honest answer: **execution first, refinement second.** Get the foot-walk happening, THEN dial the timing. Tell him this explicitly — he'll trust the order.

### Working hypothesis on level
Strong intermediate engine, intermediate body — but his shoulder is the real ceiling. **Trip success = no flare-up + a few real "aha" moments on cutback decision-making**, not technical maximum.
