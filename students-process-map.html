Jonathan
8 years surfing · Shortboard · Reef break preferred
in-clinic Intermediate (style) Frontside ✓  Backside ✗

What's Working

  • Gentle, soft turns — beautiful natural rhythm
  • Reads the wave, draws long lines
  • Patient — waits for the wave to come to him
  • Frontside flow is strong
  • Carver work translating to water

What's Blocking

  • Looking for power down instead of behind (rotation)
  • Wave selection — picking sections too steep
  • Backside pop-up mental block (not physical)
  • Reverts to "attack" mode under pressure
  • Body tightness — self-acknowledged
Progression Roadmap

Starting Point

Solid intermediate with beautiful natural style. Ceiling is mental and tactical — not physical. Backside is the obvious growth axis.
1
Wave Selection & Positioning
4m of wall about to close = walk away. He wants the section where ~1m is breaking next to him. Days 1–3: less maneuvers, more "where to sit, when to take it." Historical weakness — top priority.
"Take the friendly entry."
2
Rotate Back — Not Crouch
When the wall fades, the instinct is to crouch looking for power. Wrong direction. Rotate back toward the whitewater with toe-side pressure on the back foot — BEHIND him on a point break, not in front.
"Power isn't down. Power is the spin back."
3
Backside Pop-Up — Mental Release
Physical capacity is fully there. The work is releasing the hands, committing the jump, trusting the landing. Land drill: 10 backside pop-ups before each session — last 3 with eyes closed to practice trust. In-water: keep on easier waves until the release is automatic.
"Release the hands. Jump. Trust where you land."
4
Middle Band Flow
Soft turns, lots of them, between upper and lower sections. Small smooth arcs. The "boring, unimpressive" feel of this work IS the path — longer wave time breeds comfort, comfort breeds mastery.
"Below the top, above the bottom — boring is the goal."
5
Carver Between Sessions
Reinforces flow and backside mechanics between water sessions. Use during rest periods. Especially valuable for backside off-the-top reps. He already says it's helping — lean into it.
6
Daily Mobility & Tightness Work
Non-negotiable. He self-diagnosed the tightness — "I'm just an hour into something tai-chi people have done for 200 years." Honor that with patience and daily stretching before and after sessions.

Trip Goal

Backside pop-up unlocked + first confident off-the-top

Day 1 — 3 Focus Points

  1. Power isn't down — rotate back to the foam (don't crouch)
  2. Take the friendly entry, not the 4m wall
  3. Small smooth arcs in the middle band
T
Tali
6 years surfing · CJ Nelson + Pyzel · Flow is the north star
in-clinic Intermediate (intuitive) Flow = north star

What's Working

  • Intuition for the wave — knows where to go
  • Beautiful wave choice and soft pop-up
  • "Come back to the wave" instinct is active
  • Clean entry and strong wave awareness

What's Blocking

  • Compression without extension — no upward release
  • Back hand drifts behind the hip (pulls off balance)
  • Feet not adjusted before turning back to foam
  • Intuitive cues drop out under pressure
Progression Roadmap

Starting Point

Naturally fluid intermediate. Does the right things by feel — job is to bring intention to what she already does intuitively.
1
Compress Like a Spring — Then Extend
Knees bend deep at the bottom, then push the chest up at the top. The energy is being compressed but never released upward. Drill: call out "down" and "up" on 5 consecutive turns so the rhythm becomes conscious.
"Down-and-up like a spring."
2
Back Hand Stays at the Hip
Imaginary line running down the hip — back hand never crosses it. When the hand drifts behind, the body pulls backward and balance is lost. Visual that landed: "Someone pulling your hand back = falling backward."
"Imaginary line — back hand never crosses it."
3
Feet Move BEFORE the Turn Back
Sequence: back foot adjust → front foot adjust → THEN initiate the turn to the foam. Turning without repositioning first completely kills the return flow. This is the piece that unlocks the reconnect.
"Don't turn until your feet are set."
4
Build Conscious Checkpoints
She does the right things intuitively but loses them when the pressure is on. Brief land conversations before each session to anchor cues consciously — so they survive the heat of the wave.
5
Cross-Step Drills on the CJ Nelson
She explicitly asked for this. Start dry-land on the glider, then progress to water. Frame it honestly: "destroys flow at first, then locks in beautifully." 1–2 year body-rewiring project — start the clock now.
6
Inside Section Confidence (stretch goal)
She defaults wider/outside when scared. Gradually move her closer to the inside on smaller sets, then larger. Her sweet spot is "a little scared and succeeding" — push when she stalls, don't let her settle outside.

Trip Goal

Linked turns with intentional flow + cross-stepping foundation started

Day 1 — 3 Focus Points

  1. Down-and-up like a spring — compress then extend
  2. Back hand stays at the hip, always
  3. Move feet before turning back to the foam

in-clinic Injury protocol Intermediate (theory-rich)

Injury-first: Shoulder tendinosis — long-term physio. Cap intensity at ~5/10. Day 1 session was 7/10 — already too high. Shorter sessions (1.5h max), long rests, patient wave selection (1 wave per 15–20 min is fine). Mandatory stretch after every session. Hold shortboard until late-trip, if at all. Be the brake — he will push past pain.

What's Working

  • Strong speed generation
  • Good wave eye and selection
  • Compression piece is there
  • High theory knowledge — understands the moves

What's Blocking

  • One-trick rhythm — same line every wave
  • Energy stops at the bottom (no extension up)
  • Feet too far forward — turning options choked
  • Doesn't recognize when to wait vs turn back
  • Shoulder injury is the real ceiling
Progression Roadmap

Starting Point

Strong intermediate engine locked in one rhythm. Theory very high, execution below it. Shoulder is the actual ceiling — trip success is no flare-up + 2–3 conceptual unlocks.
0
Always-On: Injury Management
Cap intensity ~5/10. Sessions max 1.5h. Long rests between. Patient wave selection — 1 wave per 15–20 min is absolutely fine. Mandatory stretch after every session (do the first one with him so it sticks). No shortboard until end of trip, if at all.
1
Decision-Making on a Flat Wall
When the wall goes flat he keeps running. Wrong move. Pause. Turn back. Reconnect to the foam. Drill: commit to one deliberate turn-back per session rather than running through a dead section.
"When it dies, go get it back."
2
Walk Feet Back Before the Turn
Stance is too far forward — all turning options are choked. Back foot slides back, front foot adjusts, BEFORE the turn. He asked "when do I move my feet?" — answer: execution first, then refine timing. Tell him this explicitly, he'll trust the order.
"Tap-tap fast, then turn."
3
Release Energy UP Through Extension
After the bend: chest rises, knees extend, hands push up. Energy is currently trapped at the bottom — scraping the hand and pushing through the toes keeps it stuck. This directly serves his speed-preservation goal.
"Compress, then send it up."
4
Longboard / Glider for Cutback Practice
Gives him the time and forgiveness to feel the cutback rhythm properly. Also reduces shoulder load while maximising learning per session. Best tool for introducing the turn-back concept.
5
Commit Hard on the Double-Ups
Best walls on offer. He's working hard on them but not committing the angle. These are highest-value waves — when one appears, lean in fully and stick to it.

Trip Goal

No shoulder flare-up + first real cutback decision + speed preserved through a full wave

Day 1 — 3 Focus Points

  1. Cap intensity ~5/10 — mandatory stretch after every session
  2. When the wall dies, turn back to the foam — don't keep running
  3. Tap-tap feet back BEFORE turning

Michal
Mid-length board · High drive · Pre-clinic call pending
in-clinic Intermediate (driven) Pre-clinic call pending

What's Working

  • Compression-extension on entry — finds the energy zone
  • Strong drive and full engagement
  • Speed generated in the highest-energy section

What's Blocking

  • Foot order reversed: back-then-front (years of muscle memory)
  • Tunnel vision: fights closeouts instead of choosing an exit
  • Tension shuts down movement options mid-wave
  • Off-the-lip and cutback not yet attempted — waves are offering them
Progression Roadmap

Starting Point

Intermediate with strong drive — bottlenecked by one years-old motor pattern. Everything downstream unlocks once the foot order rewires.
1
Flip the Foot Order: FRONT First, Then Back
The single highest-leverage change this trip. She has been moving back-then-front for years — everything downstream depends on this rewire. Non-negotiable: 10 minutes of land reps EVERY session before paddling out. No skipping. This IS the program.
"Front first."
2
Read the Closeout — Choose an Exit
Three real options when a section closes: (a) escape with speed off the back, (b) go UP for an off-the-lip, (c) cutback back to the foam. Stop fighting the section — use one of the doors.
"Stop fighting — choose a door."
3
First Off-The-Lip Attempt
The waves are giving her perfect setups. She is ready. Frame it as: "When you'd otherwise bail, send it up instead." This is the stretch goal but it's genuinely within reach on this trip.
4
Relax to Access Movement
When tense and over-bent, no movement option is available. The mid-length has three stance positions — she can't play between them while locked up. Loose feet and soft hands must come before any attack move.
"Loose feet, soft hands — THEN attack."
5
Break Tunnel Vision — One Breath Out
She locks into whatever she's chasing and misses the situation changing around her. One breath out before committing = enough pause to see what the wave is offering and pick the right response.
"One breath out, then go."

Trip Goal

Foot order rewired + first off-the-lip attempted + cutback unlocked

Day 1 — 3 Focus Points

  1. Front foot FIRST — 10 min land drill before every session, no exceptions
  2. Stop fighting closeouts — choose a door (escape / off-the-lip / cutback)
  3. Loose first, then attack

Koby
4 years surfing · Beginner-intermediate · Needs external activation
in-clinic Beginner–Intermediate Fear loop active

What's Working

  • Patient and attentive to the whitewater
  • Doesn't panic — keeps trying to reconnect
  • Gets concepts verbally
  • High wipeout tolerance (key insight — fear is a habit, not a memory)

What's Blocking

  • Pop-up: front knee gets stuck on the way up
  • Drops too low on wall — bleeds all speed
  • Pressure without extension (energy trapped down)
  • Entry angle pointed at shore instead of along the wall
  • Fear loop — undefined origin, habit not injury memory
Progression Roadmap

Starting Point

Beginner-intermediate on a real physical block AND a fear loop. He has explicitly asked to be pushed — accept the invitation. Once both are addressed, he has fast upside.
1
Pop-Up Workaround — Survive the Section, Then Ride
Known front-knee limitation — work on it long-term, but don't let it block waves now. Workaround: pull harder, twist head sideways, push more with hand pressure. On a critical section: half-belly through it, then stabilize. This is a PRO move — it multiplies his wave count immediately.
"Survive the section, then ride."
2
Address the Fear Loop Directly
The meta-blocker behind everything. Key insight: he's NOT afraid of wipeouts — he's been through plenty and is fine. The fear is a habit, not a real injury memory. Land conversation first: "If you're not afraid of falling — what ARE you actually afraid of?" Let him sit with it. Then be his external voice in the water.
"Go."  "Take it."  "Don't hesitate."
3
Stay in the Upper Portion of the Wall
He drops to the bottom and loses all speed — every cycle. Stay middle/top. Never dig to the bottom. Staying high gives him the height needed to drive back down for a cutback — the move he wants most.
"Don't dig to the bottom — climb from the middle."
4
Soft Entry Angle — Along the Wall, Not at the Beach
Entry angle currently points directly at shore, killing all options to climb back up. Approach the wall at a gentle angle running along it, then climb from there. This is the posture that makes a cutback geometrically possible.
"Go ALONG the wall, not AT the beach."
5
Compress Then Extend — Not Just Press
Knee bend → body rises (chest up, hands lift). Currently pressing through toes only — energy trapped down, no speed generated. He thinks "go lower" is the answer — it isn't. Timing and extension is.
"Tall back, busy knees."
6
Soften the Turns
Less hard turning, softer pressure changes — so he stays connected to the wall instead of bouncing off it. Smooth gradual arcs rather than abrupt pivots keep the energy flowing.
7
Weight Distribution — Shoulders Open, Knees Do the Work
He believes the answer is "go lower." It isn't. Open shoulders + correct timing. Back stays straight, knees move. Correct every time it appears — the misconception is deeply ingrained.

Trip Goal

8 enjoyable waves per session + first real cutback + fear loop named and worked

Day 1 — 3 Focus Points

  1. Survive the section any way possible — then ride
  2. Stay upper wall, don't dig to the bottom
  3. Approach the wall at an angle, not straight at the beach
